David versus Goliath.
How a shepherd boy defeated a giant
It is one of the best-known stories in the Bible. Here it is, just as Scripture tells it.
Chapter One. David is anointed.
The Lord rejected King Saul and sent the prophet Samuel to Bethlehem, to Jesse.
Samuel saw Jesse's eldest son, Eliab, and thought: surely this is the Lord's anointed.
For man looketh on the outward appearance, but the Lord looketh on the heart. First Samuel, chapter sixteen, verse seven.
Jesse brought seven of his sons before the prophet, but the Lord had chosen none of them.
There remaineth yet the youngest, and, behold, he keepeth the sheep.
David was the youngest of Jesse's eight sons.
Then Samuel took the horn of oil, and anointed him in the midst of his brethren: and the Spirit of the Lord came upon David from that day forward.
The name David most likely means: beloved.
Chapter Two. The giant of Gath.
The Philistines gathered their armies. Israel stood on a mountain opposite, with a valley between them.
Out of the Philistine camp came a champion named Goliath, of Gath.
Whose height was six cubits and a span.
He wore a helmet of brass and a coat of mail, and his spear's head weighed six hundred shekels of iron.
He challenged Israel: Give me a man, that we may fight together.
King Saul and all Israel were greatly afraid.
Chapter Three. A shepherd with a sling.
His father sent David to the camp with bread for his brothers.
David heard the giant defy the armies of the living God, and he offered to go out against him.
Saul doubted: David was only a youth.
David answered that while he kept the sheep, the Lord had delivered him from the lion and the bear.
He trusted that the Lord would deliver him from this Philistine too.
Saul put his own armour on him, but David took it off: he had not proved it.
And he took his staff in his hand, and chose him five smooth stones out of the brook, and put them in a shepherd's bag.
Chapter Four. In the name of the Lord.
Goliath saw the young man and despised him.
Thou comest to me with a sword, and with a spear, and with a shield: but I come to thee in the name of the Lord of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel.
The Lord saveth not with sword and spear: for the battle is the Lord's.
David slung a stone, and the stone sank into the giant's forehead.
Goliath fell on his face to the earth. When the Philistines saw it, they fled.
The shepherd won not by weapons, but by faith in God.
Chapter Five. After the victory.
After the victory over Goliath, David became a national hero and a commander in Saul's army.
Saul's son Jonathan became his faithful friend.
But envy grew in King Saul's heart. That is another story.
Later David became king of Israel.
From his line came the Anointed One whom Israel awaited, Jesus Christ.
